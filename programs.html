Program 01 · SIC 63990

Information and research

The value in this work is rarely the answer; it is knowing how much weight the answer will bear. So anything supplied comes with its provenance and with the parts that could not be established, because a confident summary that conceals a gap is worse than no summary at all. A client making a decision needs to know which of three facts is solid and which is an inference.

Program 02 · With corrections

Publishing

A publisher is judged less by its mistakes than by what happens after one. So the correction route is decided before publication rather than improvised under pressure: who can be told, how quickly a change is made, and whether a correction is marked as one. Quietly editing a page and hoping nobody kept a copy is the cheapest option available and the one that costs the most when it is noticed.

  • Businesses and organisations

Program 03 · What it brings with it

Letting users post

A comments box is two days of development and a permanent set of obligations, and the order in which people discover that is usually the wrong way round. The questions are concrete: can one user see another's content, is there any route for a child to reach it, what would a risk assessment have to say, and who inside the organisation is going to own it. Sometimes the honest answer is that the feature is not worth what it brings.

Program 04 · Routes that work

Reporting and takedown

Most small services have no reporting route at all, or one that reaches an inbox nobody reads. The practical fix is unglamorous: a visible way to report, a named person who receives it, a log of what was decided and when, a stated timescale, and a route for the person whose content was removed to object. The log matters more than people expect, because a decision nobody recorded is a decision nobody can show they made.

  • Set up once
  • Services with user content
  • Logged decisions
  • Timescales stated
